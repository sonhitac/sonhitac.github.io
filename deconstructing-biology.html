Exhibition, July 2023

Deconstructing Biology

A solo exhibition at the Toronto Public Library that used bold shapes, patterns, and colour to make molecular-level biology approachable and playful for visitors with no scientific training.

Nine works, based on my journal-cover illustrations and other illustrations inspired by molecular biology, hung at the Oakwood Village Library and Arts Centre branch from July 1 to 31, 2023, as part of the Toronto Public Library's Art Exhibit Space program. Each work was paired with a plain-language write-up explaining the molecular science behind it for a general audience. I brought the show to the community where I grew up, to broaden public understanding of molecular biology there.

Visitor responses

From the guestbook

Comments left in the guestbook at the Oakwood branch suggest the work reached people outside the scientific community in the way it was designed to.

"What a beautiful way to merge science & art together to create such beautiful colourful art. Loved it."

"A wonderful idea to merge art and science to inform both to your audience."

One visitor, who said they lacked the background knowledge to follow the science, was still fascinated by the colour combinations and left a single word: "Clarity." Another wrote that they wished they had learned this material in their biology classes.

Wall text

A sample write-up: Dendritic Delivery

Each work hung above a short plain-language explanation. This is the one for Dendritic Delivery (2023, digital art):

"This piece illustrates the findings of a study published in the journal Med. Our immune system helps us fight infections and cancers. Here, the immune cell dendritic cell (in pink) produces the protein CCL19 (represented by boxes in the rainbow). CCL19 helps activate the immune cell T cell (in blue) to help it fight breast cancer. This cover art was released during Pride Month. The rainbow celebrates Pride and LGBTQ+ visibility."
