Research

I study how visual and conceptual metaphor shapes comprehension of biomedical concepts: how a diagram's simplifications, a syllabus's framing, or a graphical abstract's visual choices determine who can actually understand and trust the science being communicated.

Research direction

The question, and where it comes from

The question

Every simplification, a diagram's, a syllabus's, a graphical abstract's, is a choice about who gets to understand what's being communicated, and who doesn't.

Where it comes from

This question grows directly out of two earlier stages of my career. During my PhD in molecular and cell biology, I researched the role of cyclic nucleotide-gated channels (CNGCs) in plant immune responses. In the years since, as a science editor evaluating manuscripts for clarity, accuracy, and impact, and as a commissioned illustrator translating research into journal covers and public-facing art, I've seen from multiple angles how much a "finished" piece of scientific communication assumes about its audience without anyone deciding so on purpose.

How I treat it

My research program treats representation, including what an image assumes about a viewer's prior knowledge and identity, as something to test and evaluate, not just notice.
